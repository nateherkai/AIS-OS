## SECTION 1: NATIONAL FFA FARM BUSINESS MANAGEMENT CDE OVERVIEW

### Event Purpose

The Farm Business Management CDE is designed to teach management skills and critical and analytical thinking. It encourages and fosters the education of students in business management and teaches students to:

- Analyze and respond to questions about agricultural businesses and business principles
- Use business management and economic principles
- Analyze farm records and management resources
- Consider risk management practices

### Event Format

#### A. Individual Activity (Multiple Choice Test)

- **Time**: 90 minutes per team member
- **Format**: 50-question multiple-choice test taken individually
- **No communication** between participants during this section
- **Allowed**: Pencils, acceptable calculator (basic functions only), scratch paper
- **Scoring**: 6 points per question = 300 points max per person
- **Team Score from Individual**: Top 3 individual scores count (900 points max)

#### B. Team Activity (Problem-Solving)

- **Time**: 75-90 minutes (varies by state)
- **Format**: Team completes 25 open-ended problems collaboratively
- **Evaluation**: Judges observe teamwork during activity
- **Scoring**:
  - Teamwork evaluation: 100 points
  - Answer sheet: 200 points
  - Total team activity: 300 points

#### C. Scoring Summary

| Section                   | Points   | Notes              |
| ------------------------- | -------- | ------------------ |
| Individual (top 3 scores) | 900      | 300 pts x 3 people |
| Team Problem Solving      | 300      | Includes teamwork  |
| **Total Possible**        | **1200** | **Perfect score**  |

### Eligibility & Rules

- Open to grades 9-12 agricultural education students
- Four-member team (all four scores count)
- **No graphing or programmable calculators**
- **No cell phones** (including calculators)
- Only concepts from **National FFA Career Development Handbook** are tested
- **Total time limit**: 3 hours

### Tiebreaker Process

1. Most correct answers on 10 pre-selected questions
1. Highest problem-solving team score
1. Highest score on highest-scoring team

## SECTION 2: FARM BUSINESS MANAGEMENT CORE CONCEPTS

### Economic Principles Related to Business Management

#### 1. Production Functions

**Definition**: Show the relationship between inputs and outputs

- **Diminishing Returns**: As inputs increase from zero, outputs increase at an increasing rate, then at a decreasing rate, then reach maximum, then decline
- **Example**: Adding water to corn increases yield, but at some point adds diminish returns; too much water floods the field
- **Marginal Revenue**: Additional revenue from selling one additional unit of output
- **Marginal Cost**: Additional cost incurred from producing one additional unit of output
- **Profit Maximization**: Occurs where marginal revenue equals marginal cost

#### 2. Opportunity Costs

**Definition**: The highest-valued alternative that must be foregone when a choice is made

- **Example**: If growing wheat vs. corn, opportunity cost of wheat = value of corn foregone
- Applies to resources including labor and investment opportunities
- Critical for enterprise selection decisions

#### 3. Cost Analysis

**Types of Costs**:

- **Fixed Costs**: Do NOT vary with output level (interest, property taxes, insurance, depreciation)
- **Variable Costs**: CHANGE with output level (feed, labor, seed, fuel)
- **Total Costs** = Variable Costs + Fixed Costs
- **Average Costs**: Total cost ÷ Units produced
- **Marginal Costs**: Additional cost per additional unit produced

**Key Formula**:

```text
Total Costs = Variable Costs + Fixed Costs
Average Cost = Total Cost ÷ Number of Units
Marginal Cost = Change in Total Cost ÷ Change in Quantity
```

#### 4. Enterprise Selection

**Specialization**:

- Focus all production on one commodity
- Use when producer has comparative advantage
- Generally increases total farm profits

**Diversification**:

- Produce more than one commodity
- May decrease total profits BUT decreases risk
- Protection against total revenue loss from single commodity disaster
- Example: Corn + turkeys = revenue from turkeys if hail destroys corn

#### 5. Supply and Demand

**Law of Supply**:

- **Direct relationship** between price and quantity supplied
- As price increases → quantity supplied increases
- As price decreases → quantity supplied decreases
- Graphically: Upward sloping curve
- **Elasticity of Supply**: How much quantity changes when price changes
  - Elastic = large change in quantity (flatter curve)
  - Inelastic = small change in quantity (steeper curve)

**Law of Demand**:

- **Inverse relationship** between price and quantity demanded
- As price increases → quantity demanded decreases
- As price decreases → quantity demanded increases
- Graphically: Downward sloping curve
- **Elasticity of Demand**: How responsive quantity is to price change
  - Elastic = sensitive to price changes
  - Inelastic = insensitive to price changes

**Equilibrium**:

- Occurs where quantity supplied = quantity demanded
- **Surplus**: When market price > equilibrium price (quantity supplied > quantity demanded)
- **Shortage**: When market price < equilibrium price (quantity demanded > quantity supplied)

**Substitute & Complementary Goods**:

- **Substitutes**: Serve same purpose (cheeseburgers vs. chicken sandwiches)
  - Price increase in one → demand increases for other
- **Complements**: Consumed together (peanut butter + jelly)
  - Price increase in one → demand decreases for other

#### 6. Types of Markets

**Perfect Competition (Price-Taker Markets)**:

- Many small sellers
- Homogeneous (identical) products
- No barriers to entry
- Price-takers: Must accept market price
- Examples: Wheat, eggs, milk (commodity agriculture)

**Monopolistic Competition (Competitive Price-Searcher Markets)**:

- Many sellers
- Differentiated products (distinguished by quality, design, branding)
- Low barriers to entry
- Price-searchers: Set own prices
- Examples: Specialty cheese, organic vegetables (producers differentiate to get higher prices)

**Oligopoly Markets**:

- Small number of sellers
- Homogeneous or differentiated products
- Price-searchers who must consider competitor reactions

**Monopoly Markets**:

- One seller
- Unique product with no good substitutes
- High barriers to entry
- Price-searcher: Sets prices
- Usually regulated by government (utilities)

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL ANALYSIS & STATEMENTS

### Financial Statement Basics

#### Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Condition)

**Purpose**: Snapshot of financial situation at ONE point in time
**Formula**: Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity (Net Worth)

**Current Assets** (Convert to cash within 1 year):

- Cash, checking/savings accounts
- Accounts receivable (money owed to business)
- Crops and feed inventory
- Feeder livestock
- Government payments
- Prepaid expenses

**Non-Current (Fixed) Assets** (Held for ongoing business):

- Machinery, equipment
- Breeding livestock
- Land, buildings
- Vehicles

**Current Liabilities** (Due within 1 year):

- Accounts payable
- Credit card balances
- Accrued interest and taxes
- Short-term loans (≤12 months)
- Portion of long-term loans due within 12 months

**Non-Current (Fixed) Liabilities** (Due after 1 year):

- Long-term loans for land, machinery, equipment
- Long-term breeding livestock loans

#### Income Statement (Profit & Loss Statement)

**Purpose**: Shows net income (profit/loss) over a period (usually 1 year)
**Measures**: Revenue and Expenses

**Revenue**:

- Income from production commodity/output
- Accrual adjustments include:
  - Value of products produced but not sold
  - Products sold but payment not received
  - Reflected on balance sheet as changes in accounts receivable, inventory

**Expenses**:

- Costs to produce revenue
- Accrual adjustments include:
  - Costs incurred but not paid
  - Depreciation (expense on non-cash basis)
  - Reflected on balance sheet as accounts payable

**Formula**:

```text
Net Farm Income = Revenue - Expenses
```

**Basis of Accounting**:

- **Cash Basis**: Record when cash is received/paid
- **Accrual Basis**: Record when earned/incurred (more accurate for farm business)

#### Statement of Owner's Equity (Statement of Net Worth)

**Purpose**: Shows source of changes in net worth over a period

**Formula**:

```text
Ending Net Worth = Beginning Net Worth
                 + Net Income
                 + Non-Business Cash Inflows
                 - Owner Withdrawals
                 + Asset Valuation Changes
                 + Contributed/Distributed Capital
```

#### Statement of Cash Flows

**Purpose**: Shows cash inflows and outflows over a period
**Useful for**: Cash flow projections to ensure adequate cash for operations

**Three Sections**:

1. **Operating Activities**: Cash from farm operations
1. **Investing Activities**: Purchase/sale of assets (machinery, land, livestock)
1. **Financing Activities**: Loans received, debt repayment, dividend payments

**Interpretation**:

- **Positive Cash Flow**: Inflows > outflows (shown as increase in cash on balance sheet)
- **Negative Cash Flow**: Outflows > inflows (may require operating loan or vendor credit terms)

## SECTION 4: FINANCIAL STATEMENT RATIOS

### Solvency Ratios (Ability to repay all debt if assets sold)

**Debt/Asset Ratio** = Total Liabilities ÷ Total Assets

- Shows liabilities relative to assets
- Higher ratio = higher financial risk for business
- **Desirable**: Below 0.50
- Interpretation: At 0.40, creditors have $0.40 per $1.00 of assets

**Debt/Equity Ratio** = Total Liabilities ÷ Total Equity

- Measures borrowed capital vs. owner capital
- **Preferable**: Less than 1.0 (owner has more invested than creditors)
- Example: 0.75 = $0.75 owed for every $1.00 of equity

**Equity/Asset Ratio** = Total Equity ÷ Total Assets

- Shows what portion of assets financed by owner
- **Desirable**: Above 0.50
- Complement to Debt/Asset Ratio

### Liquidity Ratios (Ability to pay short-term obligations)

**Working Capital** = Current Assets - Current Liabilities

- Capital available after paying current liabilities
- **Desirable**: Positive working capital
- Shows cash available for operations/purchases

**Current Ratio** = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

- Relationship between current assets and liabilities
- **Desirable**: Above 1.5
- **Warning**: Too high may reduce profitability
- Example: 2.0 = $2.00 in current assets per $1.00 in current liabilities

### Profitability Ratios (Profit relative to resources used)

**Net Farm Income** = Revenue - Expenses

- Dollar figure showing farm profitability
- Foundation for other profitability calculations

**Return on Assets (ROA)** = (Net Farm Income + Farm Interest - Unpaid Labor Value) ÷ Average Total Assets

- Rate of return on all farm assets
- Shows how efficiently assets generate profit
- **Interpretation**: 5% ROA = farm earns $5 profit per $100 in assets

**Return on Equity (ROE)** = (Net Farm Income - Unpaid Labor Value) ÷ Average Total Equity

- Rate of return on owner's equity investment
- Most important profitability measure for owner

**Operating Profit Margin Ratio** = (Net Income + Farm Interest - Unpaid Labor) ÷ Gross Revenue

- Returns to capital relative to gross revenue
- Shows profit percentage of total revenue

### Repayment Capacity Ratios (Ability to repay debt)

**Debt/Income Ratio** = Average Total Liabilities ÷ Net Farm Income

- Farm debt relative to income generating ability
- Shows how many years of income needed to repay all debt
- Example: 5.0 = 5 years of all income needed to repay debt

### Financial Efficiency Ratios

**Asset Turnover Ratio** = Gross Revenue ÷ Average Total Assets

- How efficiently assets create revenue
- Higher ratio = more efficient use of assets

**Operating Expense Ratio** = (Operating Expenses - Depreciation) ÷ Gross Revenue

- How efficiently operating expenses create revenue
- Lower ratio = more efficient

**Labor Productivity Ratio** = Gross Revenue ÷ (Labor Expenses + Unpaid Labor Value)

- How efficiently labor creates revenue
- Higher ratio = more productive labor use

## SECTION 5: BUDGETS & PLANNING

### Types of Budgets

**Enterprise Budget**:

- Expected returns and costs for ONE production activity
- Example: Corn, cattle, dairy operation
- Time period: Usually 1 year or production cycle
- Includes receipts, operating costs, fixed costs

**Whole Farm Budget**:

- Expected returns and costs for ALL enterprises combined
- Shows total farm financial picture
- Time period: Usually 1 year
- Used for overall planning and decision-making

**Partial Budget**:

- Expected revenues and expenses from a CHANGE in farm operation
- Used to evaluate one specific change (switch enterprises, buy equipment, etc.)
- Focuses only on items that change

### Budget Components

**Receipts**: Income from commodity production

**Operating Costs**: Costs directly tied to production

- Seed, fertilizer, fuel
- Labor, repairs
- Feeder animals, feed
- Medicine, supplies
- Marketing costs

**Fixed Costs**: Costs incurred regardless of production

- Property taxes
- Depreciation on machinery/buildings
- Property insurance
- Interest on loans

## SECTION 6: RISK MANAGEMENT

### Four Types of Agricultural Risk

#### 1. Financial Risk

**Sources**: Poor financial status, fluctuating interest rates, economic changes, reduced asset values

#### Financial Risk Management Techniques

- Monitor financial statements to understand strength/weaknesses
- Obtain fixed-rate loans to avoid interest rate fluctuations
- Rent/lease equipment or land to avoid debt
- Own land to reduce landlord/lease agreement risk
- **Insurance**:
  - Life insurance: Protects family and business from death
  - Property insurance: Protects buildings, vehicles, equipment
  - Health insurance: Covers injuries/illness impact
  - Liability insurance: Protects from legal risks

#### 2. Production Risk

**Sources**: Weather (drought, frost, fire), disease, insects/pests, weeds

#### Production Risk Management Techniques

- **Crop Insurance** (USDA Risk Management Agency):
  - Common Crop Insurance: Losses from production decrease and/or price
  - Vegetation Index: Pasture, rangeland, forage, apiculture
  - Adjusted Gross Revenue-Lite: Whole farm revenue protection
  - Livestock Risk Protection: Decline in livestock market prices
  - Livestock Gross Margin: Loss of margin from price/feed cost changes
- **Diversification**: Multiple enterprises protect against total loss
- **Excess Capacity**: Equipment/facilities to handle emergencies
- **Share Leases**: Risk sharing with landlords
- **Government Programs**: SURE, ACRE, disaster assistance

#### 3. Marketing (Price) Risk

**Sources**: Commodity price volatility, supply/demand uncertainty, global factors

#### Marketing Risk Management Techniques

- **Storage**: Hold commodities when prices low, sell when favorable
- **Forward Contracts**: Lock in future sale price (protects against price decline, but loses upside)
- **Futures Contracts**: Protection against price changes (obligation to buy/sell at set price)
- **Options**: Right (not obligation) to buy/sell at set price
- **Insurance**: Crop/livestock insurance products
- **Government Programs**: Price supports, disaster assistance

#### 4. Institutional/Legal Risk

**Sources**: Government policy changes, contract disputes, tax implications, succession issues

#### Institutional Risk Management Techniques

- **Estate Planning**: Prepare for farm transition to avoid tax/succession problems
- **Business Organization**:
  - Sole Proprietorship: Unlimited personal liability
  - Partnership: Unlimited joint liability
  - Corporation: Limited liability
  - LLC: Limited liability hybrid
- **Written Agreements**: Document all contracts in writing with signatures
- **Knowledge**: Understand laws affecting agricultural business

#### 5. Human Resource Risk

**Sources**: Illness, injury, death, poor communication, lack of training

#### Legal and Institutional Risk Management Techniques

- **Organization**: Define roles, responsibilities, authority structure
- **Training Programs**: Adequate employee training reduces accidents/inefficiency
- **Hiring Practices**: Comprehensive job descriptions, interview process
- **Employee Handbook**: Policies on compensation, benefits, conduct, leave
- **Succession Plan**: Plan for business transition to next generation

## SECTION 7: ECONOMICS TERMINOLOGY (Key Definitions)

### Accounts

Basic storage unit for accounting data; accumulates similar transactions

### Accounts Payable

Claims against the farm business not yet paid

### Accounts Receivable

Amounts owed to the farm business for products/services

### Accrual Accounting

Revenue/expenses included in period earned/incurred, regardless of payment timing

### Amortization

Repayment of loan with series of equal payments over time

### Assets

Economic resources controlled by the farm business

### Average Output/Yield

Total units of output ÷ number of units of input

### Break-Even

Output level where revenue equals total costs (fixed + variable)

### Budget

Schedule of expected returns and costs

### Capital

Non-human resources used in production (buildings, machinery, equipment, cash)

### Capital Budgeting

Process of estimating investment profitability; comparing alternatives

### Capital Gain/Loss

Gain/loss from selling capital asset for more/less than adjusted basis

### Cash Flow Statement

Shows cash received and spent during accounting period

### Comparative Analysis

Comparison of expected results from different actions/approaches

### Depreciation

Allocation of capital asset cost over its useful life; decrease in value due to wear/obsolescence

### Diversification

Engaging in several enterprises to protect against risk or fully use resources

### Economies of Scale

Ratios of inputs remain same as output increases

### Economies of Size

Total cost per unit decreases as business size increases

### Enterprise

Specific process/activity producing single output

### Enterprise Budget

Shows expected returns and costs for one production activity

### Equity/Net Worth/Owner Equity

Total assets - total liabilities

### Fixed Assets

Assets with life greater than one year

### Fixed Costs

Costs that do NOT vary with production level

### Fixed Input

Factor of production quantity that doesn't vary with output

### Leverage

Use of borrowed money to increase assets

### Liquidity

Ability to convert assets to cash quickly

### Marginal Cost

Additional cost from producing one additional unit

### Marginal Revenue

Additional revenue from selling one additional unit

### Net Worth

Value of business = Assets - Liabilities

### Operating Expenses

Costs directly tied to production (seed, feed, fuel, labor)

### Opportunity Cost

Highest-valued alternative foregone when choice is made

### Price Elasticity of Demand

% change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price

### Price Elasticity of Supply

% change in quantity supplied ÷ % change in price

### Profit

Revenue minus all expenses

### Return on Assets (ROA)

Net income ÷ average total assets (as a percentage)

### Return on Equity (ROE)

Net income ÷ average total equity (as a percentage)

### Revenue

Income from production commodity/output

### Risk

Uncertainty about outcomes; variability in results

### Solvency

Ability to repay all debt if all assets sold

### Variable Costs

Costs that CHANGE with production level (feed, labor, seed)

### Working Capital

Current assets - current liabilities

## SECTION 10: QUESTIONS STUDENTS MUST ANSWER ON CDE

Based on national standards, questions typically cover:

1. **Financial Analysis**

   - Interpret balance sheets
   - Calculate financial ratios
   - Analyze cash flow
   - Evaluate profitability

1. **Decision-Making**

   - Compare enterprise options
   - Calculate break-even points
   - Evaluate profitability scenarios
   - Recommend business changes

1. **Risk Analysis**

   - Identify risk sources
   - Recommend risk management strategies
   - Evaluate insurance options
   - Assess financial vulnerability

1. **Production Economics**

   - Apply supply/demand concepts
   - Calculate costs (fixed, variable, average, marginal)
   - Analyze production efficiency
   - Evaluate market conditions

1. **Business Planning**

   - Develop budgets
   - Create financial projections
   - Plan for business growth
   - Evaluate business organization
