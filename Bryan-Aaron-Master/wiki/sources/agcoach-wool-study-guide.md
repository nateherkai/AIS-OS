# Ag Coach Pro — Wool and Mohair Evaluation Study Guide

Comprehensive CDE study guide for the Wool FFA contest event. Texas produces ~20% of total US wool clip and >97% of total mohair clip, contributing ~$50M/year to state agricultural income.

## Wool Grading Systems

### American Grade System (6 grades by fineness)
Fine, 1/2 blood, 3/8 blood, 1/4 blood, Low 1/4 blood, Braid

### Bradford Spinning Count (Numerical System)
More accurate; spinning count = max hanks (560 yd/hank) per pound.

| American Grade | Spinning Count | Micron Range |
|---|---|---|
| Fine | 64's–80's | <17.70–22.04 |
| 1/2 blood | 60's–62's | 22.05–24.94 |
| 3/8 blood | 56's–58's | 24.95–27.84 |
| 1/4 blood | 50's–54's | 27.85–30.99 |

### Length Classifications (within each fineness grade)
- **Staple** — longest, suitable for Noble combs, highest price
- **French Combing** — medium length, French comb compatible
- **Clothing** — short, unsuitable for combing, lowest price

Length minimums for Staple grade: Fine ≥3", 1/2 blood ≥3.5", 1/4 blood ≥4", Braid ≥4.5".

## Wool Yield and Shrinkage

- **Shrinkage** = % foreign material in grease wool (dirt, sand, vegetable matter, yolk)
- **Yield** = % clean wool after scouring
- **Yolk** = wool grease + dried perspiration
- Texas average yield for fine wool: 50%. High-yield: >53%; low-yield: <45%

## Secondary Wool Characteristics

- **Soundness** — fiber strength throughout; "tender wool" = weak everywhere; "break" = specific weak spot
- **Purity** — freedom from kemp (opaque, brittle fibers) and colored fibers
- **Character** — general appearance + crimp (natural waviness); distinct even crimp = strength
- **Color** — bright/creamy white preferred; stains limit use to dark fabrics

## Key Mohair Evaluation Factors

- **Staple Length** — ideal 1"/month (6" per 6-month shearing)
- **Luster** — brightness/shininess; high luster = highly preferred
- **Softness (Handle)** — harshness is undesirable
- **Condition** — ideal shrinkage ~15%; excessive oil discriminated against
- **Purity** — freedom from kemp and colored fibers

## Contest Mechanics

- **Classification** — grade individual samples
- **Placing** — rank groups of four fleeces
- **Commercial Classes** — based on greatest economic return (pounds clean wool); vegetable matter + strength heavily weighted
- **Breeding Classes** — focus on heritable traits; strength/vegetable matter often disregarded; emphasis on purity + breed character

## Key Glossary Terms

- **Break** — specific weak point caused by fever, feed changes, or severe weather
- **Crimp** — natural waviness/curl of fiber
- **Kemp** — opaque, brittle, chalky white fiber; serious defect; does not dye well
- **Noils** — short fibers removed during combing
- **Tags** — heavy dung-covered locks
- **Top** — continuous band of long parallel combed fibers with noils removed
